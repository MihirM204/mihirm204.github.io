The Case for Paper

A speech on writing by hand in a digital world

By Mihir Mahale · Speech

Good evening, everyone!

In an era where we are constantly surrounded by devices that buzz, flash, and ping us, competing for our attention, it might seem surreal to talk about something as simple as writing on paper. With laptops, iPads, and smartphones at our fingertips, it's easy to think that these advanced digital tools are all we need in our day-to-day academic and creative lives.

But despite all these breakthroughs in technology, today, I'd like to explore why writing by hand remains an incredibly effective tool in our fast-paced, connected digital world. Whether it's for note-taking, brainstorming, or expressing your feelings.

Memory Retention and Understanding

Let's begin with learning. Writing by hand has been proven to impact memory retention, understanding, and comprehension. A 2014 research titled: The Pen is Mightier Than the Keyboard: Advantages of Longhand Over Laptop Note Taking by Pam Mueller of Princeton University and Daniel Oppenheimer of University of California, Los Angeles showed that students who take handwritten notes instead of typing them have a more remarkable ability to recall and conceptualize information.

When you write notes on paper, you actively engage your brain, forcing it to sift information and focus on what is important. Writing takes time, so you are compelled to process the information more extensively, developing stronger cognitive connections this promotes better understanding and long-term retention. It's like creating a mental map—one that's refined and optimized for your thinking.

Flexibility, Creativity and Focus

A 2010 study by Anne Mangen (University of Stavanger, Norway) and a 2011 study by Karin James (Indiana University) shows that writing by hand increases creativity and focus. The process of writing is slower, but that's precisely what facilitates a freer flow of ideas. As your pen glides across the page, ideas unfold more naturally, giving you room to think, draw connections, and express yourself without the constraints of a structured digital interface.

The paper doesn't come with distractions. There are no notifications or social media tabs calling your name. You're entirely in the moment, allowing your mind to stay clear and focused empowering creativity to flourish.

Kevin Systrom, co-founder of Instagram, initially conceptualized the app through pen-and-paper sketches, which allowed him to visualize the user interface and core functionality before the development process began. This shows how early-stage brainstorming can benefit from the flexibility of paper.

A Break from Screens

Let's be honest: most of us spend much of our day staring at screens. Our eyes and brains are constantly bombarded with digital content, whether for classes, studying, or leisure. Writing by hand provides a practical opportunity to rest the eyes from digital screens, which has been linked to reduced digital eye strain. When you write on paper, you steer clear of the blue light and digital fatigue that results from staring at a screen.

Personalization, and Precision

Unlike the fixed formats of a laptop or tablet, pen and paper allows for a high degree of customization in note-taking. Whether you're doodling in the margins, drawing diagrams, or color-coding your concepts, writing by hand will enable you to craft notes to reflect your learning style and understanding, making the material more personal.

As the author Gillian Flynn once said, "There is something deeply satisfying about writing by hand. You can't delete it and start again without consequence." Handwriting makes us more deliberate and thoughtful, ensuring that each stroke on the page carries meaning and purpose. There’s permanence in writing by hand, which demands our focus and attention in a way that Technology often doesn't.

The Pleasure of Writing

Finally, there's the simplistic pleasure of scribbling by hand. It engages different sensory and cognitive processes, providing a tangible connection to the work. Feeling the texture of the paper, and watching your ideas come to life in real time is an experience that feels more human and timeless. It's an opportunity to create in a grounded, and meaningful way.

Conclusion

I'm not suggesting we abandon our laptops or iPads—those tools are necessary for our academic and creative endeavors. But writing on paper has its own set of benefits. While digital tools allow for faster note-taking writing on paper enhances memory retention, sparks creativity, helps us focus, and provides a refreshing break from the digital world. It's a tool that allows us to work precisely and express ourselves freely.

As Steve Jobs once said, "Technology alone is not enough—it's Technology married with the liberal arts, married with the humanities, that yields us the results that make our hearts sing. It reminds us that the best tools aren't just efficient—they're meaningful and allow us to engage more deeply with our work.

Thank you, everyone. Have a great evening.
