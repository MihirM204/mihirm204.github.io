Outlook

What I believe about technology.

Six ideas I keep coming back to. Each one comes from something I’ve written.

1
“Judgment is mine to keep.”

I use AI as a brainstorm, not a prescription. Polished language can look like truth when it isn’t, so I check it against my own experience.

2
“AI can be very sharp and very blind at the same time.”

Machines don’t get tired, but they can’t read the driver who’s in a hurry. Context is still a human skill, and good systems are designed around that.

3
“In a digital world flooded with information, clarity is kindness.”

If someone can’t understand an interface, it doesn’t matter how powerful or beautiful it is. Simple language is a form of respect.

From my HCI coursework on readability
4
“Risk management is not a one-time calculation. It is an ongoing moral obligation.”

The Big Dig and the I-35W bridge failed long after the math was done. Engineers owe people continuous, honest scrutiny.

5
“These technologies are not simply tools for communication. They are tools for humanity.”

The best technology adapts to the person, never the other way around. Voice cloning for people with ALS keeps who they are, not only how they talk.

6
“Respect is the minimum, not the goal.”

Localization, accessibility and inclusive design belong at the start of a project. They’re an ethical commitment, not a last-mile feature.

From my HCI coursework on inclusive design

Cryptography · Try it

A bio-inspired cipher.

My encryption algorithm hides a message in plain sight. It looks like a real genomic sequence, it’s shuffled with the same golden-angle spiral sunflowers use to pack seeds, and it can tell when someone has tampered with it. Type below. Everything runs in your browser.

  1. 1SubstitutionYour key seeds a random-number generator. Its keystream is XORed against the message bytes.
  2. 2Codon encodingEach byte becomes four bases from A, C, G and T, since 4⁴ = 256. The output mimics a real genome.
  3. 3Phyllotaxis scatterCodons are reordered by the golden angle, 137.5°. It’s deterministic, so no permutation table is stored.
  4. 4Integrity markerA checksum byte is folded in first, so a single changed base is caught on decode.
